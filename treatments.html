Pharmacological agents

—
Agent Class Indication Mechanism Efficacy Key adverse effects Cost
Disease-modifying era

Anti-amyloid mAbs (lecanemab, donanemab) are the first agents shown to slow clinical progression of early AD. Both require Aβ confirmation (PET or CSF) and MRI ARIA monitoring.

Symptomatic agents (ChEIs, memantine) remain standard of care across stages and dementia subtypes.

ARIA monitoring

For lecanemab/donanemab, MRI surveillance at baseline + before doses 5, 7, 14 (donanemab also 22, 26).

APOE ε4 homozygotes carry highest ARIA risk — counsel and consider risk-benefit.

Avoid in dementia
  • Anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, oxybutynin)
  • Benzodiazepines (falls, paradoxical effects)
  • Z-drugs (zolpidem)
  • Tricyclic antidepressants
  • First-gen antipsychotics for BPSD

See AGS Beers Criteria 2023.

Non-pharmacological interventions — matched to disease stage

First-line for behavioral symptoms · always pair with pharmacotherapy

What works at each stage is different. Below: the strongest-evidence non-drug interventions at each phase, with how to deliver them, who provides them, and what to expect. Non-pharmacological approaches are first-line for behavioral symptoms (NICE 2018, APA 2024).

Preclinical · No symptoms
Build cognitive reserve
Goal: delay or prevent symptom onset

Top-evidence interventions

FINGER multidomain programA
Diet + exercise + cognitive training + vascular monitoring. WW-FINGERS replicated globally; US arm is U.S. POINTER. wwfingers.com
MIND / Mediterranean dietA
Highest-adherence groups had 53% lower AD risk in Rush MAP study. Berries, leafy greens, nuts, fish, olive oil; limit red meat & sweets.
Aerobic + resistance exerciseA
≥150 min/wk moderate intensity. Improves hippocampal volume and executive function. Brisk walking, swimming, dancing, cycling.
Hearing-loss treatmentA
ACHIEVE trial 2023: hearing aids cut 3-yr cognitive decline 48% in high-risk older adults. Largest single modifiable risk factor.
Sleep hygiene + OSA treatmentB
7–8 hrs/night; treat sleep apnea (CPAP). Poor sleep impairs glymphatic clearance of amyloid.
Social + cognitive engagementB
Volunteering, classes, languages, music. Loneliness is an independent risk factor (Lancet 2024).
MCI / Predementia
Slow conversion to dementia
Daily life independent; mild objective cognitive change

Top-evidence interventions

Cognitive training (computerized)A
ACTIVE trial: 5 weeks of speed-of-processing training cut dementia risk over 20 years. Apps like BrainHQ, Constant Therapy.
Continued exercise (escalated)A
Both aerobic and resistance. Group-based programs improve adherence. Tai chi for fall prevention + cognition.
Mediterranean / MIND dietA
Continue from preclinical phase. Treat any new metabolic abnormalities.
Reversible-cause workupA
Always rule out: hypothyroidism, B12 deficiency, depression, sleep apnea, anticholinergic medication burden, normal-pressure hydrocephalus. ~5% are reversible.
Driving evaluationB
Formal on-road test through OT or AAA Senior Driving. Some states have anonymous physician reporting.
Legal & financial planningB
Do this NOW while capacity is intact. POA, healthcare proxy, advance directives. Free state forms at caringinfo.org.
Hearing & vision optimizationA
Update glasses; treat cataracts; fit hearing aids if not yet done. Sensory loss accelerates cognitive decline.
Mild dementia · CDR 1
Preserve function
Help with complex tasks; daily basics still independent

Top-evidence interventions

Cognitive Stimulation Therapy (CST)A
Group-based 14-session program. Cochrane 2023: significant improvement in cognition and quality of life. NICE-recommended first-line.
Reminiscence therapyB
Structured discussion of past memories with photos, music, objects. Improves mood and engagement.
Occupational therapy (TAP)A
Tailored Activity Program: OT designs activities matched to abilities. Reduces caregiver burden by 40% (NIH-funded RCT).
Adapted exercise programsB
Walking groups, chair yoga, dance therapy. Continue at moderate intensity with supervision.
Caregiver education / support groupA
REACH II program reduced caregiver depression and delayed nursing home placement by 22 months. Alzheimer's Association support groups are free.
Home safety modificationsB
Remove throw rugs, install grab bars, lock medications, ID bracelet, GPS tracker if wandering risk emerging.
Adult day programB
Structured engagement 2–5 days/week. $80–150/day. Provides caregiver respite + social engagement.
Moderate dementia · CDR 2
Manage behaviors · maintain dignity
Needs help with bathing, dressing; behavioral symptoms common

Top-evidence interventions

Music therapyA
Cochrane 2024 meta-analysis of 22 RCTs: significant reduction in agitation, depression, anxiety. Personalized playlists from earlier life.
DICE framework for behaviorsA
Describe / Investigate triggers / Create plan / Evaluate. Endorsed by AAGP and Alzheimer's Association. First-line for agitation, wandering, sundowning.
Bright light therapyB
Morning exposure (2,500–10,000 lux for 30–60 min). Improves sleep-wake cycle, reduces sundowning.
Pet therapy / doll therapyB
Reduces agitation and apathy. Robotic pets (PARO seal) effective when live animals not feasible. Doll therapy controversial but evidence-supported.
Snoezelen multisensory roomsC
Soothing lights, gentle sounds, tactile objects. Mixed evidence; helpful for individuals with severe agitation in some studies.
Validation therapyB
Acknowledge emotion behind a confused statement rather than correcting facts. Reduces distress for both patient and caregiver.
Memory care / structured residentialB
Secured units with dementia-trained staff. Look for low antipsychotic prescribing rates (<14%) and high engagement programming.
Caregiver respiteA
In-home aide or short-stay respite (3–14 days). CMS GUIDE program offers $2,500/yr respite benefit through participating practices.
Severe dementia · CDR 3
Comfort · presence · dignity
Total dependence; minimal language; swallowing impaired

Top-evidence interventions

Hand massage / gentle touchA
Reduces agitation, lowers cortisol. 5–10 min sessions. Family or staff can deliver. Strong evidence in late-stage care.
Familiar music (continued)A
Recognition of familiar music persists very late. Music & Memory program; iPod with personalized playlist.
Comfort feeding / hand feedingA
Prefer over feeding tubes (no benefit on survival or aspiration; AGS, AAFP recommendations). Small bites, finger foods, high-contrast plates.
Hospice referralA
Eligible at FAST stage 7c (limited mobility, recurrent infections, weight loss). Medicare covers in home, facility, or inpatient.
Pressure injury preventionA
Reposition every 2 hrs; specialty mattresses; meticulous skin care. Prevents painful, infection-prone wounds.
Aromatherapy (lavender, lemon balm)B
Lavender for agitation; lemon balm cream for hand massage. Modest but consistent effect; very low risk.
Spiritual care · presenceB
Visits, prayer, hymns, reading aloud. Familiar voices recognized even when speech is gone. Bereavement planning for family.
Avoid hospitalizationA
Hospital admissions in late dementia rarely improve outcomes; treat in place when possible. Have advance directive on file.
EVIDENCE GRADES
A = Strong RCT support / multiple meta-analyses B = Moderate evidence / single good RCT C = Limited or mixed evidence
For physicians: these interventions should be considered before pharmacotherapy for behavioral symptoms (per NICE 2018, APA 2024 Practice Guideline). Antipsychotics carry a 1.6–1.7× mortality boxed warning in this population.
For caregivers: talk to your loved one's clinician about which of these interventions are available locally. Many are covered by Medicaid HCBS waivers, VA caregiver benefits, or the new CMS GUIDE dementia care program. See Community Care.

⚠ Antipsychotic boxed warning

All antipsychotics in older adults with dementia carry a 1.6–1.7× mortality risk. Use only when behavior poses safety risk; reassess every 4–6 weeks; trial gradual dose reduction.

DLB — high sensitivity

Patients with DLB or PDD can develop severe extrapyramidal reactions to typical antipsychotics. Quetiapine or pimavanserin preferred if antipsychotic required.

FTD-specific

ChEIs not effective and may worsen behavioral symptoms in bvFTD. SSRIs (sertraline, citalopram) are first-line for behavioral disinhibition and compulsions.

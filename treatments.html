Pharmacological agents

Disease-modifying era

Anti-amyloid mAbs (lecanemab, donanemab) are the first agents shown to slow clinical progression of early AD. Both require Aβ confirmation (PET or CSF) and MRI ARIA monitoring.

Symptomatic agents (ChEIs, memantine) remain standard of care across stages and dementia subtypes.

ARIA monitoring

For lecanemab/donanemab, MRI surveillance at baseline + before doses 5, 7, 14 (donanemab also 22, 26).

APOE ε4 homozygotes carry highest ARIA risk — counsel and consider risk-benefit.

Avoid in dementia
  • Anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, oxybutynin)
  • Benzodiazepines (falls, paradoxical effects)
  • Z-drugs (zolpidem)
  • Tricyclic antidepressants
  • First-gen antipsychotics for BPSD

See AGS Beers Criteria 2023.

First-line for behavioral symptoms

Per NICE and APA 2024 — non-pharmacological approaches are first-line for agitation, apathy, sleep disturbance, and wandering.

Pharmacotherapy reserved for safety risk or refractory symptoms after a trial of behavioral approaches.

DICE framework
  1. Describe the behavior
  2. Investigate triggers
  3. Create a plan
  4. Evaluate response

Kales et al., 2014; endorsed by AAGP and Alzheimer's Association.

FINGER replication

Multidomain (diet+exercise+cognition+vascular) replicated globally as WW-FINGERS network spanning 60+ countries.

US arm: U.S. POINTER (Alzheimer's Association).

⚠ Antipsychotic boxed warning

All antipsychotics in older adults with dementia carry a 1.6–1.7× mortality risk. Use only when behavior poses safety risk; reassess every 4–6 weeks; trial gradual dose reduction.

DLB — high sensitivity

Patients with DLB or PDD can develop severe extrapyramidal reactions to typical antipsychotics. Quetiapine or pimavanserin preferred if antipsychotic required.

FTD-specific

ChEIs not effective and may worsen behavioral symptoms in bvFTD. SSRIs (sertraline, citalopram) are first-line for behavioral disinhibition and compulsions.

The journey of dementia — from silent biology to symptoms

Patients · families · clinicians

Dementia does not start the day someone forgets a name. The brain changes 15 to 20 years before any symptoms appear, then passes through a quiet warning phase, before the most familiar dementia symptoms emerge. Understanding where someone is on this journey helps with planning, prevention, and treatment.

Stage 1 · The Silent Years

What's happening in the brain

  • Amyloid-β plaques begin building up
  • Tau tangles seed in deep memory areas
  • Subtle inflammation and brain shrinkage start

Causes & risk drivers

  • Age — risk doubles every 5 years after 65
  • APOE ε4 gene — 3× risk per copy
  • Midlife blood pressure, cholesterol, hearing loss, head injury
  • Sleep & social — chronic poor sleep, isolation

What can you do now?

  • Manage blood pressure, LDL, diabetes
  • Treat hearing loss; stay socially engaged
  • MIND/Mediterranean diet · 150 min exercise/week
  • Discuss family history with primary care
Stage 2 · The Warning Years
Predementia (MCI)
2–7 yrs typical duration Mild Cognitive Impairment — daily life still independent
10–15%
Per year convert from MCI to dementia
24–27
Typical MMSE range

What you might notice

  • Forgetting recent conversations or appointments
  • Repeating questions; losing familiar items
  • Word-finding pauses; new anxiety about memory
  • Friends or family quietly notice the change

Causes

  • Same drivers continuing — but now with measurable cognitive change
  • Always rule out reversible mimics: thyroid, B12, depression, sleep apnea, medication side effects
  • ~5% of dementia-like presentations are reversible at this stage

What to do now

  • See a neurologist or memory clinic for evaluation
  • Get blood test: plasma p-tau217 if available; CBC, B12, TSH
  • Consider MRI; amyloid PET if changing course of treatment
  • Complete legal & financial planning while capacity is intact
  • Eligible window for anti-amyloid therapy (lecanemab/donanemab) if AD-type

The most common causes

  • Alzheimer's disease — 60–70%; amyloid + tau pathology
  • Vascular dementia — 5–10% (often mixed with AD)
  • Lewy body dementia — 5–15%; Parkinsonism + visual hallucinations
  • Frontotemporal dementia — most common cause under 65

What changes through the stages

  • Mild: Loses items, gets lost driving, struggles with finances
  • Moderate: Needs help bathing, dressing; may not recognize family; wandering, sleep changes
  • Severe: Total dependence; minimal language; difficulty swallowing

Care now matters most

  • Build a care team: clinician, social worker, family
  • Match level of care to stage — see community services
  • Non-drug approaches first for behavior — see stage-by-stage guide
  • Caregiver support is part of the treatment plan
Prevention works at every stage. The Lancet Commission 2024 estimates that addressing 14 modifiable risk factors could prevent or delay up to 45% of all dementia worldwide.

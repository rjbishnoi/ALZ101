Cognitive Risk Screener

Pattern recognition · Educational use
Not diagnostic

ALZ101 Risk Assessment

12 ITEMS · ~3 MIN

A multi-domain risk-pattern tool combining demographic, lifestyle, cognitive, and behavioral factors. Generates pattern signals (AD / vascular / Lewy body / FTD) and recommends next steps. Educational only — formal diagnosis requires clinician evaluation with validated instruments.

When to Screen
Annual: All adults 65+ at Medicare AWV

Targeted:
  • Subjective memory concern
  • Family history
  • Functional decline
  • Mood / personality change
  • Down syndrome (annual ≥40)
Differential Patterns
  • Memory + functional→ AD
  • Stepwise + vascular RFs→ VaD
  • Hallucinations + RBD→ DLB
  • Behavior, younger age→ FTD
  • Gait + UI + cog→ NPH
  • Mood predominant→ Depression
Reversible Mimics

Always rule out:

· Hypothyroidism
· B12 / folate deficiency
· Depression
· Medication side effects
· Sleep apnea
· NPH
· Subdural hematoma
· Neurosyphilis (RPR)

Cognitive domains — what each one is and how it's tested

7 DOMAINS · WITH PATIENT-FACING EXAMPLES

Cognitive screening tests aren't a single thing — they sample distinct mental abilities. Different dementias damage different domains first. This is why one person can be a "memory" patient and another a "language" or "behavior" patient. Below, the 7 cognitive domains used in modern neuropsychology (per DSM-5 and the AAN), with definitions and concrete bedside examples.

DOMAIN 1
Orientation
Awareness of who you are, where you are, and when. Often the first to fail in delirium and AD.
How it's tested
"What is the date today?" "What floor are we on?" "Where do you live?"
What impairment looks like
Saying it's the wrong year by months or years; not knowing the city; thinking the clinic is a different place.
Hit early in
Alzheimer's disease; delirium; Wernicke-Korsakoff
DOMAIN 2
Attention
The ability to sustain focus, ignore distractions, and hold information briefly in mind. Highly sensitive to delirium and DLB.
How it's tested
Digit span (repeat back 5 numbers); months of the year backward; serial 7s (subtract 7 from 100, repeat).
What impairment looks like
Losing place mid-task; forgetting what they walked into a room for; can't follow a multi-step instruction.
Hit early in
DLB (fluctuating attention is a core feature); delirium; vascular dementia
DOMAIN 3
Memory
Forming, storing, and retrieving information. Episodic memory (events) is hit hardest in AD; semantic memory (facts) and working memory (holding info in mind) are separable.
How it's tested
3-word delayed recall (apple / table / penny — 5-minute delay); story recall; word-list learning.
What impairment looks like
Repeating questions within minutes; forgetting recent conversations; missing appointments — but old memories often spared early.
Hit early in
Alzheimer's disease (the prototypical memory-first dementia); semantic-variant primary progressive aphasia (semantic memory)
DOMAIN 4
Language
Producing and understanding speech and writing. Includes naming, fluency, comprehension, repetition, reading, and writing.
How it's tested
Naming (point to a watch, lion, glasses); fluency (animals in 1 minute — normal ≥18); repetition; comprehension of complex sentences.
What impairment looks like
Word-finding pauses (anomia); circumlocution ("the thing for time" instead of "watch"); short, halting sentences.
Hit early in
Primary progressive aphasia (PPA); semantic-variant FTD; later-stage AD
DOMAIN 5
Executive function
Planning, organizing, sequencing, abstract thinking, judgment, problem-solving. Controlled by the frontal lobes.
How it's tested
Trail Making Test B (alternating numbers and letters); clock drawing (executive components); proverb interpretation; similarities ("How are an apple and orange alike?").
What impairment looks like
Trouble managing finances or medications; impulsive decisions; getting overwhelmed by multi-step tasks; poor judgment.
Hit early in
Vascular dementia; FTD (behavioral variant); Parkinson disease dementia; subcortical dementias
DOMAIN 6
Visuospatial / perceptual-motor
Perceiving objects in space, their relationships, depth, and using visual information to guide movement.
How it's tested
Clock drawing (place numbers, set hands to 11:10); cube copy; intersecting pentagons; line bisection.
What impairment looks like
Getting lost in familiar places; trouble parking the car or going down stairs; bumping into doorways; difficulty reading.
Hit early in
Posterior cortical atrophy (PCA); DLB; later AD
DOMAIN 7
Social cognition
Recognizing emotions, understanding social cues, behaving appropriately, regulating impulses. Often the first to fail in behavioral-variant FTD.
How it's tested
Reading-the-Mind-in-the-Eyes test; faux-pas recognition; emotion-naming from photos; clinician-observed behavior + family report.
What impairment looks like
Saying inappropriate things in public; loss of empathy; impulsive spending; eating from others' plates; new sexual behaviors.
Hit early in
Behavioral-variant FTD — the cardinal early signs are social-cognitive
Why this matters
A "low MoCA score" is just the headline. The pattern across these 7 domains is what tells your clinician whether the cognitive change looks more like Alzheimer's, vascular disease, FTD, or DLB. That, in turn, drives biomarker testing, imaging, and treatment choices. If you've had a screening test, ask which domains were affected — not just the total.

Primary Care Pathway

Step 1 · Trigger

Subjective concern (patient or informant), age 65+ AWV, family history, or functional change.

Step 2 · Brief screen

Mini-Cog or GPCOG (≤5 min). If concerning, proceed to Step 3.

Step 3 · Extended cognitive assessment

MoCA + informant questionnaire (AD8 or IQCODE). Score MoCA <26 with informant report → likely cognitive impairment.

Step 4 · Workup for reversible causes

CBC, CMP, TSH, B12, folate, RPR. Depression screen (PHQ-9 or GDS-15). Medication review.

Step 5 · Imaging

Brain MRI (preferred) or CT. Look for atrophy patterns, vascular disease, NPH, mass lesions.

Step 6 · Refer or treat

Refer to neurology / memory clinic if: rapid progression, atypical presentation, age <65, anti-amyloid candidacy.

Specialty / Memory Clinic Protocol

Comprehensive evaluation

  • Detailed history with informant
  • Neuropsychological battery (memory, executive, language, visuospatial)
  • CDR / CDR-SB staging
  • Functional assessment (FAQ, ADL/IADL)
  • Behavioral assessment (NPI)

Biomarker workup

  • Plasma p-tau217 (high NPV for excluding AD)
  • If AD suspected: amyloid PET or CSF Aβ42/p-tau ratio
  • If DLB suspected: DaT-SPECT, polysomnography (RBD)
  • If FTD: structural MRI for frontotemporal atrophy; consider FDG-PET
  • Genetic counseling if early-onset / strong FH

Anti-amyloid eligibility

  • MCI or mild AD (CDR 0.5–1)
  • Confirmed Aβ pathology
  • APOE genotyping (ε4/ε4 high ARIA risk)
  • MRI exclusion: ≥4 microhemorrhages, superficial siderosis, macrohemorrhage
  • No anticoagulation if avoidable
